01 · Business Problem
The Problem
ShopNest Global is a large-scale e-commerce platform operating in 30+ countries with 50M+ active customers and 2,000+ human support agents working 24/7 across the US, Europe, India, and Southeast Asia, processing 200,000+ orders/day. Every order carries risk of delivery delay, payment failure, wrong item, or return — and each generates a support ticket. Real tickets are messy: some overloaded with emotional detail, some shorthand-heavy, some a single sparse sentence. Agents spend 2–3 minutes per ticket just decoding the issue before resolution work starts, and peak periods spike volume from ~5,000/day to ~15,000/day.
The Solution
A 4-stage n8n workflow powered by GPT-4.1 that summarizes raw tickets into clean, factual summaries, drafts a professional and policy-grounded customer response with a clear next step, and evaluates both outputs automatically with LLM-as-a-Judge scoring — giving the business measurable, auditable quality signal before a broader rollout decision.

Stage 1
Ticket Summarization
GPT-4.1 · temp 0.2 · max 150 tokens
System Prompt Rules
Summarize in 2–3 concise sentences. Clearly identify the main issue. Remove unnecessary emotional language. Preserve important facts. Mention order/payment/delivery/refund issue explicitly. Maintain factual accuracy. Do not hallucinate.
Input / Output
Input: raw support_ticket_desc field. Output: clean 2–3 sentence summary, stored as Summarization.
Stage 2
Summary Evaluation (LLM-as-a-Judge)
GPT-4.1 · temp 0.2 · max 150 tokens
Rubric
Accuracy — does the summary correctly reflect the original ticket? Clarity — is it easy to understand and professional? Completeness — does it include all major issues, requests, and context? Each scored /10, plus short feedback. Penalizes hallucinations and missing detail.
Judge Input
Original ticket text + the Stage 1 summary, so the judge can compare directly. Output format constrained to plain text (no markdown).
Stage 3
Response Generation
GPT-4.1 · temp 0.4 · max 250 tokens
System Prompt Rules
Be empathetic and professional. Acknowledge the customer's issue. Provide clear next steps. Maintain a calm, respectful tone. Keep it concise and actionable. Do not make unsupported promises.
Policies Embedded in Prompt
Refund & Return, Delivery Delay, Wrong Item, and Payment Failure policies are hard-coded directly into the system prompt (see Overview tab) so every generated response is grounded in the same rules, not model guesswork.
Stage 4
Response Evaluation (LLM-as-a-Judge)
GPT-4.1 · temp 0.1 · max 180 tokens
Rubric
Professionalism, Empathy, Clarity, Policy Compliance (does it correctly follow the stated policies?), Actionability (clear, useful next steps). Each scored /10, plus short feedback. Penalizes unsupported promises and vague actions.
Why a Lower Temperature
temp 0.1 for the judge (vs. 0.2–0.4 for the generation stages) — evaluation needs to be consistent and objective run to run, not creative.
Design Notes
Why n8n?
No-code orchestration made the 4-stage pipeline visually debuggable node-by-node, and the Set node pattern (Prepare Final Output) made it trivial to assemble all 6 output fields — ticket id, description, summary, summary eval, response, response eval — into one row per ticket before CSV export.
Why Two Separate Judge Calls?
Summarization and response generation are different tasks with different failure modes (factual drift vs. policy compliance), so each gets its own rubric and its own LLM-as-a-Judge call rather than one combined evaluation — keeping each score interpretable and actionable on its own.

Key Finding
Policy Compliance was the lowest and most variable metric (min 7/10). The judge repeatedly flagged responses that promised a specific outcome — a full refund, a Rs 100 voucher — without the ticket containing enough information to confirm eligibility against the stated policy. This is the clearest lever for prompt iteration: constrain Response Generation to confirm eligibility criteria before offering compensation, rather than offering it proactively.
